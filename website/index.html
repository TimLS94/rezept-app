How it works

Four things, done properly

Not a recipe database. A kitchen assistant that knows what is in your fridge, how many people are eating, and what you said you cannot eat.

📸

Scan your fridge

Photograph the shelves. SpoonDrop reads what is in there and ranks recipes by how little you would still have to buy — not by how popular they are.

🥚 eggs🧀 cheese🍅 tomatoes🧅 onion
🔗

Save a reel

Share an Instagram post into SpoonDrop and it reads the caption, the text on screen and what the cook says out loud — then writes the recipe down. Screenshots and photographs of a cookbook page work too.

👨‍👩‍👧

Cook for who is actually eating

Tell it who lives with you once. Every quantity scales — a child counts as half a portion — and the shopping list adds up across the whole week instead of per recipe.

🍳

Cook it, step by step

Full-screen steps with timers where the recipe needs them, so the phone stops being something you keep unlocking with wet hands.

Honest by default

We tell you when we are guessing

Nutrition figures worked out by a model are labelled as estimates wherever they appear — a badge on the recipe, a tilde on the card. Anything not labelled came from the person who wrote the recipe. You are deciding what to eat; you should know which is which.

Allergy warnings show, they never silently hide a recipe. And a recipe you import stays in your own cookbook — private, yours, and never republished under someone else's name.
